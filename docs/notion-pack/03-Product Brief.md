# Product Brief

## Overview

We are building a `careerOS / job intelligence product` for job seekers.

The product helps users discover more relevant jobs than platforms like LinkedIn surface, apply stronger filters, understand actual job requirements, and prioritize what to pursue next.

## Problem

Job seekers face several recurring problems:

- Platforms do not show all relevant jobs consistently
- Filters are weak or misleading
- Platform labels often do not match actual job requirements
- Job feeds optimize for platform behavior, not user outcomes
- Users waste time evaluating low-fit or stale opportunities
- There is little transparency around why a job is shown, hidden, or prioritized

## Product Promise

Give job seekers a clearer, more complete, and more actionable view of the job market than existing platforms provide.

## What Makes This Product Different

- Better recall than feed-based job platforms
- Stronger and more flexible filtering
- Explainable ranking and prioritization
- Better understanding of the actual job, not just the platform metadata
- Potential for multi-source dedupe and shared job intelligence
- A path toward becoming a full careerOS, not just a job tracker

## Target User

Primary:

- Active knowledge-worker job seekers frustrated by LinkedIn and similar platforms

Secondary:

- Career switchers
- Technical/professional users managing a high-volume search
- Users who want more control over job discovery and prioritization

## MVP Scope

In:

- Saved search monitoring
- Job ingestion from supported sources
- Better-than-LinkedIn filtering
- Basic ranking/prioritization
- Explainable reasons for why jobs are shown or filtered
- Desktop-first experience

Out:

- Full coaching product
- Marketplace/recruiter tools
- Deep collaborative features
- Overly broad source support before quality is stable

## Product Principles

- Practical utility over novelty
- Transparency over black-box behavior
- Better recall and prioritization over gimmicks
- User control matters
- Ambiguity should usually favor showing a job rather than hiding it
- Trust and clarity matter if data is centralized
